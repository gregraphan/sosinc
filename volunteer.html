Foster & volunteer

A spare room is enough to save a life.

Fostering means taking a cat or kitten home and caring for them until they are ready for adoption. We supply what you need. You supply the room.

Volunteer application What fostering involves

Why fostering matters

Becoming a foster home for homeless cats or kittens can be rewarding and fulfilling. It gives the cat an opportunity to live in a home environment, because living in even the best shelter is stressful for any animal.

Young kittens deserve to have their early childhood in a home setting, until they are spayed or neutered and ready for adoption. Senior cats deserve to live their days stress free.

What we learn while they are with you

During the kitty’s stay in a foster home we can learn whether they are good with children and other animals, what their personality is like, and whether they have any special needs.

Some cats need extra attention, training, special medical treatment, or socialization. That is easier to see in a living room than in a cage.

Fostering, in a home rather than a cage

Living in even the best shelter is stressful for any animal. A foster home lets a cat decompress, and lets us learn who they really are.

The four-part foster process

What Save Our Strays provides

Cost is the most common reason people hesitate. Here is what is covered.

  • Food and litter, if requested, kept in a central location for pick up
  • A cage or condo cage, if necessary
  • Medical expenses, covered by Save Our Strays
  • Vaccinations and a basic health checkup for every animal
  • Spay or neuter by an SOS veterinarian, if not already done

What we ask of you

  • If you rent, your landlord must allow pets
  • Your own cats and dogs must be current on vaccinations and sterilized
  • The cat must go to one of our veterinarians, and you will most likely transport them there and to one of the adoption outlets
  • All cats must stay inside

How fostering works

The foster home application process has four parts. It exists for the protection of the animals.

  1. The Volunteer Application

    Tell us about yourself and your household.

  2. The SOS Policies and Procedures document

    How we work, and what we expect.

  3. The Hold Harmless, Waiver and Indemnification Agreement

    Standard paperwork before any animal is placed.

  4. Home visit

    A home visit is required prior to any foster cats being placed in the home.

In an effort to serve the many cats and kittens that need our help, we are continually looking to expand our list of foster homes. If you can help, even on a temporary basis, please contact us at sos@saveourstraysinc.com or call 727-481-5262.

Volunteering, without fostering

Volunteering not only helps provide love and care to animals in need, it also gives you the chance to make a meaningful difference in your community.

It can be a rewarding way to connect with other volunteers, spend time with the kitties, build compassion, reduce your own stress, and gain valuable experience working with animals.

In addition to fostering, we need people to help with

  • Transporting cats to the veterinarian, to PetSmart, or to other fundraising events
  • Feeding, cleaning and visiting with senior and special needs cats at our facility

The volunteer process has three parts

  1. The Volunteer Application

  2. The SOS Policies and Procedures document

  3. The Hold Harmless, Waiver and Indemnification Agreement

A representative of SOS will then be in touch with you soon. Generally, volunteers must be at least 18 years of age, but there may be opportunities for those younger than 18 to help with fundraising.

We encourage you to reach out with questions or new ideas. Email sos@saveourstraysinc.com or call 727-481-5262.
